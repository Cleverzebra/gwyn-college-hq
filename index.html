Apply + SAT

Common App, essay, supplements, and the SAT where things stand

Open the Common App  The 7 essay prompts ↗

The essay is started, which is the hard part. The remaining path is short: finish a full draft, hand it to one adult reader, mark three fixes from their comments, fix them, paste it into the Common App. One 25-minute sitting at a time; the calendar tells you which day.

Supplements: add a college inside the Common App and its extra essays appear under that school's Writing section, which is the authoritative list. Add Bowdoin first, then the rest of the list as the family talk settles it.

SAT: you are registered for Sat Nov 7. That gives you 69 days of prep, and a retake cannot lower anything, because every school here that takes scores uses your best sections across dates. The timing, checked on College Board 8/30: Nov 7 scores release Fri Nov 20. Bowdoin Early Decision closes Sun Nov 15, so the new number will not be inside the application you file. It is not lost. Bowdoin says scores may be submitted at any time including after the deadline, takes self-reported scores in its Application Portal, superscores across test dates, and does not decide until mid-December.

So two things are on you, and the first one is easy to miss. On the Bowdoin application you have to answer yes to having your SAT reviewed, and that answer locks on Nov 15 even though the score arrives later. Answer no and the Nov 7 result cannot count. Second, send two sentences to admissions@bowdoin.edu asking whether a score you self-report on Nov 20 reaches the Early Decision readers before the mid-December decision. They do not publish an answer, so asking is the only way to know. Your 1520 stands on its own either way: Bowdoin's own published data puts the middle half of submitters at 1470 to 1540.

Practice that fits your week: the SAT Coach on the home computer (also in the Dock as SAT Coach). One 15-minute adaptive set on days without a drive. It picks the next step for you, so there is nothing to plan.
