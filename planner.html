                Best practices & sources

                The short version
                • Register ahead. Almost every school requires pre-registration for the tour and info session; walk-ins are hit or miss. Book at least a week or two out.
                • Build in unstructured time. Plan to stay on campus after the tour: eat in a dining hall, sit in the student center, walk the town. The official tour is a sales pitch; the leftover hour is the data.
                • Talk to real students who are not the tour guide. Two short conversations tell you more about warmth and vibe than the whole tour.
                • Read the walls. Bulletin boards, the student newspaper, and flyers show what the community actually cares about and argues about.
                • Do the interview where it counts. Where a school offers an evaluative interview (★ on the Plan tab: Bowdoin, Tufts, Swarthmore, Macalester, Hamilton), schedule it, it becomes part of the application. "Recommended/optional" ones are still good practice and demonstrated interest.
                • Debrief the same day. Memories of three campuses blur into one by the weekend. Use the Debrief tab before you drive home.
                • Two campuses a day, maximum. More than that and they stop being distinguishable.
                Demonstrated interest, the touchpoints that can carry weight

                From the counselor's June letter. Some schools track this; many top LACs say they do not (Amherst and Swarthmore explicitly do not). It still helps fit and gives you essay material.

                • Applying binding Early Decision, the strongest signal (for Gwyn, that is Bowdoin)
                • Campus visit, in person or virtual
                • Interview, if offered
                • Opening and reading the college's emails (yes, some track opens)
                • Attending a college fair and stopping at the table; attending a rep's visit to your school in the fall; a regional info session
                • Thoughtfully completing all essays, even the "optional" ones
                • Contact with your school's admission representative
                Common App essay, for the summer (2026-2027 cycle)

                The Common App opens August 1. The personal essay is 650 words max, and the seven prompts are unchanged from last year. Gwyn's through-line: bridge-building and community engagement (student council and VP, the move from a city public school into a new school, the Rwanda trip). Prompts 1, 5, and 7 fit that spine most naturally.

                1. Some students have a background, identity, interest, or talent that is so meaningful they believe their application would be incomplete without it. If this sounds like you, then please share your story.
                2. The lessons we take from obstacles we encounter can be fundamental to later success. Recount a time when you faced a challenge, setback, or failure. How did it affect you, and what did you learn from the experience?
                3. Reflect on a time when you questioned or challenged a belief or idea. What prompted your thinking? What was the outcome?
                4. Reflect on something that someone has done for you that has made you happy or thankful in a surprising way. How has this gratitude affected or motivated you?
                5. Discuss an accomplishment, event, or realization that sparked a period of personal growth and a new understanding of yourself or others.
                6. Describe a topic, idea, or concept you find so engaging that it makes you lose all track of time. Why does it captivate you? What or who do you turn to when you want to learn more?
                7. Share an essay on any topic of your choice. It can be one you've already written, one that responds to a different prompt, or one of your own design.
